Watch for

  • Asymmetry — one half doesn't match the other
  • Border — edges are ragged or blurred
  • Colour — several shades in one spot
  • Diameter — larger than a pencil eraser
  • Evolving — any change in size, shape, or feel

How OncoScan works

  1. 1

    Capture or upload

    Open OncoScan on your phone or laptop, then upload a photo or use your camera. A preview confirms the shot is clear before anything is analyzed.

  2. 2

    Image processing

    The photo is resized, normalised, and lighting-corrected, then studied by a trained model — EfficientNet, ResNet, or MobileNet — trained on the HAM10000 and ISIC datasets.

  3. 3

    A clear result

    You see a suggested type, a confidence score, and short educational tips — with a plain-language disclaimer on the same screen, every time.

  4. 4

    An interface for everyone

    Large touch targets, high contrast, and optional spoken results make the result easy to use and understand, regardless of ability.

Built on four rules

Safety first

OncoScan never claims to diagnose cancer. Every result carries a disclaimer and points you toward a professional — calmly, not alarmingly.

Privacy-aware

Photos aren't stored by default. Keeping a scan history is opt-in, requires consent, and can be deleted at any time.

Honest about limits

Accuracy drops with poor lighting, blur, unusual angles, or skin tone variation. The confidence score exists so you can weigh that.

Accessible by default

Semantic HTML, full keyboard support, screen-reader labels, high contrast, and spoken results — built in, not bolted on.

Who OncoScan is for

  • Anyone who's noticed a new or changing spot and wants a careful first look at home.
  • People who want plain guidance on when a dermatologist visit is worth booking.
  • Students and developers learning how to build health-aware AI responsibly.
  • Communities where specialist access is limited and an early nudge matters.

OncoScan is an educational awareness tool. It does not diagnose skin cancer or any other condition. Always consult a qualified doctor about a skin concern.
